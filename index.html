About

I develop mathematical and computational frameworks for understanding, inferring, and controlling higher-order dynamical systems.

My research lies at the intersection of nonlinear dynamics, network science, inverse problems, and control theory. I am particularly interested in how higher-order interactions shape collective behavior, how latent interaction structure can be reconstructed from data, and how such systems can be influenced through principled interventions.

Current research themes include:

  • Higher-order dynamics and pattern formation
  • Hypergraph reconstruction from dynamical data
  • Control of emergent behavior in complex systems

I received my Ph.D. in Applied and Interdisciplinary Mathematics from the university of Michigan, where my doctoral advisor was Pr Anthony Bloch. My research focused on higher-order dynamical systems, with particular emphasis on how interaction structure shapes dynamics, inference, and control in hypergraph-structured systems.
